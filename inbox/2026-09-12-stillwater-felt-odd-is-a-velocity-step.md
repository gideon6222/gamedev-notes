# When a motion "feels odd", print the per-frame change in its velocity at every state boundary before tuning anything, and fix a step with one continuous state (a damped spring whose velocity survives the boundary, plus an acceleration cap, because a stiff spring's first frame is a step too) rather than with a fourth ease.

**Game:** stillwater  **Date:** 2026-09-12  **Belongs in:** CRAFT.md / the feel / motion section

## What happened
Gideon's note on the cast was "the movement has felt odd", and the plan carried it as "the charge, the throw and the settle are three eases that do not share a curve". Before touching a constant it was measured: a probe (`scripts/probe_cast.gd`) sampled the rod butt's pitch every frame through a charge, a throw and the settle and printed the largest change in angular velocity between two consecutive frames, at each state boundary. The oddness was one number: at the release the rod went from 54 deg/s backwards (a linear lift) to 361 deg/s forwards in ONE frame for a 0.55 s hold, and 671 deg/s at full charge, because the throw was an ease-OUT that starts at its peak speed and shared no state with the lift. The other two boundaries were already continuous. The fix was one damped spring on the swing angle (`_spring_swing`) that every state aims at a different target - the charge while loading, the throw angle on release, rest otherwise - with its velocity kept as real state across the boundaries, and the natural frequency derived from an arrival time (`omega = 3 / (zeta * seconds)`, three time constants of the envelope) so the tunable is "seconds to arrive" and not a frequency. The spring alone was NOT enough: a stiff spring's first frame is itself a step (the acceleration is largest at t=0), so an acceleration cap (`SWING_ACCEL_MAX` 7200 deg/s², 120 deg/s per frame at 60 Hz) bounds the reversal to a ramp. After: the worst single-frame step is 120 for every hold length (the cap, by design), the throw still peaks at 278 deg/s mid-charge and 531 at full (a whip, not a lever), and the tip's overshoot past the stop is 0.1 degrees (damping 0.8 gives 1.5% overshoot). The smoke check asserts all three so that raising the cap or damping the whip away both go red.

## The rule
When a motion feels odd, measure the per-frame change in its velocity at every state boundary before tuning. Fix a step with one continuous state: a damped spring whose velocity persists across the boundary, plus an acceleration cap to bound the first frame (a stiff spring's first frame is itself a step too), rather than adding a fourth ease.

## Replaces or contradicts
Two motions sharing one variable will be shown doing each other's job (a rod that bends on the cast). Give each motion its own state.
