# When a binary control becomes a proportional one, re-measure the balance in the quantity the new control moves

**Game:** stillwater  **Date:** 2026-09-12  **Belongs in:** CRAFT.md / balance policies, TESTING.md / golden-and-bots practice

## What happened

Stillwater's fight control went from a hold-to-reel button (fifth fight) to a progressive slide (sixth fight: up cranks, centre holds, down gives line, all proportional, with the crank following the thumb through a short inertia). The land-rate table by band looked fine straight away (human bot 100/100/100/100/78/67 against the old 100/100/100/99/72/56), but three of the studio's own balance claims went red: "watching the water is worth something" (the bot that reads the run warning must lose fewer fish than the one that reads only the rod), "the reeds charge for a missed tell in time not fish", and the difficulty ladder. A new probe (`scripts/probe_dial.gd`: one deep fish, twelve seeds per bot, printing outcomes, fight length, peak tension, peak STRAIN, metres given, metres taken, and the share of the fight spent over the danger line) showed why, and none of it was visible in land rates: (1) with a proportional valve every bot surfs the danger line during a run, so the water-reader and the rod-reader tie on fish - what separates them is strain, 0.02-0.14 against 0.62-0.72, and at the old recovery rate that strain washed out between runs, so the rod-reader buzzed at the edge for two minutes and landed the fish anyway; the fix was slower strain recovery (0.025 -> 0.012 per second), not a bigger kick. (2) The "perfect" bot was giving line through the warning, paying out a metre per tell for a fish not yet pulling, and escaped a quarter of the boss fish - the perfect player losing to caution; it holds at the tell now and its valve aims just under the red with a tight span. (3) The human bot's HANDS were perfect: only its decisions lagged, its valve read the live tension, and it tied the perfect bot; giving it a felt tension lagged 0.15 s and a shove-not-trim valve put it between the perfect bot and the blind one - and a first cut with the full 0.3 s reaction lag on the hands broke eleven sturgeon in twelve, worse than the bot that ignores the water, which the "watcher never loses to the ignorer" rule forbids. (4) A held line during a run must carry the fish's whole resistance, not only the small pull term - the first cut made the middle of the slide free and every deep loss an escape. After all four, the human ladder read 100/100/100/99/76/58, within a few points of the fifth fight, and each bot failed for its own reason: the giver by escape, the blind by breaking, the human by either only in the deepest water. One claim had to change shape: "ignoring the tell must cost fish LANDED" became "cost fish LOST", because the greedy rod-reader lands as many per hour (shorter fights, more casts) while breaking more lines - which is the risk dial working, not the warning failing.

## The rule

When a binary control becomes proportional, test the new control dimensions (strain, line given per bot on one deep fish) not just land rates by band; the cost of ignoring the warning can migrate to quantities that recover between episodes. Bots need modelled hand lag (reaction time in the controller, not just decision lag) to properly represent human play.
