# An objective made impassable so it cannot be shortcut becomes a plug in the only path to the objectives behind it

**Game:** coreward  **Date:** 2026-09-12  **Belongs in:** CRAFT.md / Level and world design

## What happened
Nine objectives buried across a mining world, each a single unbreakable cell in the middle
of its own room. Unbreakable on purpose: the ritual is that you fly down to it and standing
beside it triggers it, so you cannot mine your way to the objective. But the nine sit in only
three columns - three each - and a cell that never breaks is a permanent plug in its column.
**Six of the nine became unreachable by digging down to them**, and each one reported the
depth of the objective above it: 94 m for the two under one at 95, 42 m for the two under one
at 43, 47 m for the two under one at 48. A long-play probe had been stalling on this for four
simulated hours and it read as a balance problem the whole time. The fix was to make the cell
uncuttable only while it is UNCLAIMED, and merely very hard (three times the local rock)
afterwards: the ritual is untouched, because an unclaimed one still cannot be mined and
triggers the moment you are beside it, and a claimed monument in the way is a monument you
are allowed to move.

## The rule
Anything permanently impassable that sits **inside** the route to later content will block
that content, and a "you cannot cheat your way to this" rule only needs to hold until the
thing is claimed. Make it impassable while it is the objective and passable once it is not,
and write the test that drives the player to **each** instance in turn rather than to one.

## Replaces or contradicts
Narrows this line in CRAFT.md / Difficulty and balance: "Never let a hazard take the run:
bound the worst case and test it." The same rule applies to geometry that is not a hazard at
all - unbreakable objective furniture took the whole campaign, not a run, and no worst-case
bound on a hazard would have caught it.
